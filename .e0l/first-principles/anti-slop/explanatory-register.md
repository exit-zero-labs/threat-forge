# Explanatory-register tells

Analytic writing — anything that explains why something is the way it is — has its own slop vocabulary, entirely distinct from the marketing tells in [copy.md](copy.md) and not caught by them.

> Sources: Project Postcard `docs/research/explanatory-form.md`, which develops these as failure modes of a specific editorial form. Only the generalisable register tells are carried here; the editorial research stays with that product.

The register is more dangerous than promotional prose, because **confident causal prose is exactly the voice in which an error travels furthest.** Its authority is the point, and that authority is what makes an unqualified claim damaging.

- **Explainer-voice connective filler.** "But here's the thing", "and that's where it gets interesting", "the answer, it turns out", "simple, right? well, not quite", "let's back up", "so what's going on here", "there's a catch", "it all comes down to", "and this is where X comes in", "the story starts in 1974". These simulate the *feeling* of an argument advancing while advancing nothing. Deterministically detectable.
- **Forward promises.** "We'll come back to that", "more on that shortly", "first, some context". Retention scaffolding borrowed from video, which does not survive the move to text: a reader can look down the page, so a promise to explain later reads as stalling.
- **Decorative numerals.** A quantity that lends authority without doing argumentative work. The subtractive test: remove the number — if the argument is unchanged, it was costume. More dangerous than a hollow superlative because it reads as rigour.
- **The just-so story.** A satisfying causal narrative the evidence does not support. Invisible to a claim-by-claim fact check: every sentence can be individually sourced while the causation asserted *between* them is invented. This is the defining risk of the register.
- **Monocausality.** One clean cause presented where several interact. The most seductive form of the just-so story, because the sentence is true — the exclusivity is what was invented.
- **Uncalibrated confidence.** Asserting a contested or thinly evidenced mechanism in the same flat declarative register as a well-sourced one. **Hedging where the evidence is thin is a craft obligation, not a weakness.**
- **Reflexive contrarianism.** "What everyone gets wrong about X", applied where no received idea genuinely exists. It manufactures a strawman in order to demolish it.

## Why this matters beyond prose

These tells appear in engineering artifacts as often as in published writing — in an ADR that invents a tidy causal story for a decision actually made on instinct, in a plan that asserts one cause for a bug with several, in a post-incident write-up whose confidence exceeds its evidence.

An ADR states the alternatives genuinely rejected and why. A plan states the plausible-but-wrong outcomes it is guarding against. Neither is improved by a narrative that closes more cleanly than the facts do.
